Privacy Policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026 (for Feelrun 1.3)

Feelrun is a running and trail-running app for iPhone and Apple Watch. It has no user accounts. Your workouts and health data stay on your devices, in Apple Health and, for what is said below, in your own private iCloud; they do not reach Feelrun's developer, except a level from 1 to 3 of your experience as a runner, reckoned from them on your iPhone, if you share your shoe ratings (below). Feelrun runs three small services of its own on Cloudflare, and each receives only what you choose to use it for: the heart rate you share live, end-to-end encrypted so that the service cannot read it (FollowMe, below); anonymous ratings of your shoes, if you share them, with a level from 1 to 3 of your experience reckoned on your iPhone (Gear ratings, below); and, if you connect Strava, the sign-in, which the service passes on and does not keep, with only a keyed hash of your Strava athlete number to count the runners connected (Strava, below).

What stays on your devices

  • Workouts, routes, heart rate and other data read from or written to Apple Health.
  • Workout history, analyses, coach chat, plans and your goal, stored in the app on your iPhone. Feelrun does not sync these through iCloud (routes you bring in and your gear are the exceptions; they go to your own private iCloud, below).
  • Your AI provider API key, stored in the iOS Keychain.

Feelrun's developer does not receive, collect, sell or share any of this data. The heart rate you may share live passes through Feelrun's relay end-to-end encrypted, unreadable to the developer (below). The app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs. What the developer can see is only what you choose to send: votes and ideas, and the gear ratings you share anonymously (both below).

Purchases

Feelrun Pro and the free days (30 for a new install, and 14 again with a new feature version) are taken through the App Store. Apple handles the payment; the app only asks the App Store whether Pro was bought or the free days started, and when. The developer receives no payment or personal details from it.

Optional AI features

If you choose OpenAI or Anthropic as the Coach Engine in Settings › AI, enter your own API key for it and allow data sharing when asked, Feelrun sends the request directly from your iPhone to that provider under your account. Each request is built from: workout titles, dates and summaries (type, distance, duration, climb); heart rate, pace, elevation and terrain derived from your routes (kilometre splits and profile points, not coordinates); heart-rate zones and your heart-rate threshold; training load; your goal, race details and the text of pages you ask Feelrun to read, and your training plan; weather at your workouts and the forecast for the coming days; your chat messages and notes, with any picture you add to a chat message; and your gear: each pair's name, kilometres, purpose, specs and ratings, and how they hold up (your check-ins and notes). When you ask Feelrun to look up a shoe's specs, the shoe's name goes to the provider, which may search the web for it. Raw GPS coordinates are not sent. The provider's own privacy policy governs that data. When a version of Feelrun adds a kind of data to this list (the gear in 1.3), it asks you again before sending it. You can withdraw permission at any time in Settings › AI (Share My Training Data); without it, no data is sent and Feelrun's built-in cues are used instead.

Votes and ideas (optional)

In Settings › AI (and among the ideas below) you can answer whether you would subscribe monthly to an AI coach that needs no key of your own. If you vote, your answer (yes or no) and the app's version are saved in Feelrun's public iCloud database, under the anonymous identifier iCloud gives your Apple Account for this app, so that each account has one vote. No name, email address, workout or health data goes with it. Only the developer sees the votes, as a count; the app shows you only your own. You can change or withdraw your vote there at any time; withdrawing deletes it.

Under Settings › General › Help Shape Feelrun you can vote for up to three ideas for what Feelrun could do next, and send an idea of your own. Each vote is saved the same way, as the idea's name and the app's version under that anonymous identifier, one per idea and account; taking a vote back deletes it. An idea of your own is saved with the app's version, and iCloud notes the same anonymous identifier as the one who wrote it. Write nothing in it that you would not want the developer to read: no name, email address, workout or health data goes with it unless you write it there. Only the developer reads the votes and ideas; nobody else using Feelrun sees them.

FollowMe (live sharing, optional)

If you start FollowMe, Feelrun writes your live location track, elapsed time, workout state and, on a known route, remaining distance to a temporary area in your own private iCloud database and creates a read-only link or QR code. Anyone you give the link to can follow your run in Feelrun while the workout lasts. Other Apple Health data is not part of it, and your heart rate only if you choose so (below), never through iCloud. Distance and pace shown to followers are calculated from the location track. The shared data is deleted when the workout ends (and leftovers are removed the next time you open the app); you can stop sharing at any time. If you choose the same link for every run, the link stays until you stop sharing: two minutes after each run its location track is deleted and only that run's distance, time and end time stay readable to whoever has the link, until your next run takes their place. Feelrun's developer cannot see it. Scanning a FollowMe code uses the camera; images are processed on the device and not stored. Someone following your run can send you a short message, which the coach reads to you: it is stored, encrypted with a key that only the run's link carries, in Feelrun's public iCloud database together with the name they give, cannot be read without the link, and is deleted by their app after an hour. When a run begins on a link you keep for every run, Feelrun writes a short note to Feelrun's public iCloud database so that your followers' phones can show "Ana started a hike": the name you share under, the kind of run, the start time and a tag derived from the link. It holds no location, can be found only with the link, and is deleted when the run ends. Followers who allow notifications get that alert; their phones keep an iCloud subscription for each runner they follow, removed when they forget the runner.

Heart rate in live sharing (optional). If you turn on Share heart rate (off until you do), your iPhone sends your heart rate every 10 seconds while you share a run to Feelrun's relay, a small service run by Feelrun's developer on Cloudflare. Each reading is encrypted and signed on your iPhone with keys made for that run alone. The key that opens it and the key that checks the signature go only to your followers, in the run's area of your iCloud that only people with the link can read, and their phones decrypt it there. Every reading is the same size and goes at the same rhythm, whatever your heart rate does. The relay and Cloudflare receive the encrypted reading, a random tag for the run that identifies neither you, the link nor the run, a counter, and the time of the reading, these last two in plain text. Like any web service, Cloudflare also sees the IP addresses of the phones that connect, and its network may log the addresses requested, which contain the tag. Neither Feelrun's developer nor Cloudflare can read your heart rate, and the relay itself keeps no logs. Only the latest reading is kept. Your iPhone deletes it when the sharing ends; should that not get through (without signal, say), the relay deletes it 15 minutes after the last reading anyway. Until 12 hours after the first reading the relay keeps only a hash of a random token from your iPhone, so that nobody else can write under the run's tag, the time of that first reading, and how many readings were written. You can turn it off at any time, also during a run; your followers stop seeing it within seconds.

Strava (optional)

If you connect Strava in Settings › Health › Strava, Feelrun signs in to Strava with your permission to upload activities and read your profile name. Strava's sign-in needs a secret of Feelrun's Strava application, which cannot be kept inside an app, so the sign-in goes through Feelrun's Strava broker, a small service run by Feelrun's developer on Cloudflare: your iPhone sends it the one-time code from Strava's sign-in page, or later the refresh token, and the broker adds the application's secret, asks Strava, and passes Strava's answer back to your iPhone. The broker keeps no logs and does not keep the sign-in; like any web service, Cloudflare sees the IP address of your iPhone. Strava lets only a limited number of runners connect to Feelrun, so the broker keeps one thing, only to count them: for each runner connected, a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256 under a secret of the broker's) of the Strava athlete number in Strava's answer to the sign-in, and the day of the sign-in. No name, no token, no activity and not the number itself. It also notes the day Strava last refused a runner because the places were full, with nothing about who. Before opening Strava's sign-in, the app asks the broker whether the places are full; that question carries nothing about you. The sign-in (access and refresh tokens, and your Strava name) is kept in your iPhone's Keychain, on this device only. Runs go from your iPhone straight to Strava, never through the broker: the runs you choose, or every run recorded with Feelrun if you turn on automatic upload. Each goes as a GPX file with its route, times, elevation and heart rate, its name and its kind. Which runs were sent is noted on your iPhone so none is sent twice. Once a run is on Strava, Strava's privacy policy and your Strava privacy settings govern it. Disconnect in the same place forgets the sign-in on your iPhone and asks Strava, through the broker, to end it. With it your iPhone sends the access token (renewed first if it has expired), with which the broker asks Strava for your athlete number just before ending the sign-in, and normally deletes its hash. The hash stays if you disconnect Feelrun on strava.com instead, if you disconnected from an older version of Feelrun or the sign-in could no longer be renewed, or if Strava does not answer; connecting and disconnecting again in Feelrun then deletes it. The developer does not try to tell whose a hash is, sees only how many there are, and may delete them all to count again. If the broker says the places are full, the app tells you so before Strava's sign-in and lets you try anyway.

Gear (your shoes)

In Gear you keep your pairs of shoes: their names, specs, your ratings, notes and check-ins on how they hold up, which pair you use by default for each kind of run, and a photo of a pair if you add one. You take the photo with the camera or pick it in the system's photo picker; Feelrun gets only that picture and keeps a copy without its location or other metadata. For each run Feelrun notes which pairs you wore and where in the run you changed pairs, so that it can count their kilometres. All of this is kept on your iPhone, the pairs are shown on your Apple Watch, and it is kept in your own private iCloud database, so your other devices have it. For a run, iCloud holds only the run's Apple Health identifier, the pairs, and where in the run (in metres) and when you changed pairs; no distance, route, heart rate or other Health data. Feelrun's developer cannot see any of it. Kilometres are counted on each device from Apple Health. When you delete a pair, it is deleted from your iCloud too.

NFC tags (optional). If you let Feelrun write an NFC sticker for a pair, the tag holds only a link with the pair's random identifier (https://feelrun.kocbek.eu/g/…), nothing else. Reading and writing a tag happen on your iPhone; Feelrun never locks a tag and asks before writing over one. Anyone who holds a phone to the tag can read the link, which tells nothing about you; a phone without Feelrun that opens it loads a page of this website, which is hosted on GitHub Pages and, like any website, sees that phone's IP address.

Gear ratings shared with other runners (optional)

Feelrun's ratings service is a small service run by Feelrun's developer on Cloudflare, with its own database (Cloudflare D1, not iCloud). It holds anonymous ratings of shoe models of the brands Feelrun knows, each model named only by a short key made from its brand, name and version (such as hoka-speedgoat-7-gtx).

Reading other runners' ratings. Anyone using Gear can see what other Feelrun runners say of a model, without sharing anything of their own. For a pair whose model Feelrun knows, the app asks the service for that model's key. When you add a pair, the app may ask for models whose names start like what you typed, to offer their shared specs; only when the name begins with a known brand, and then only the brand and at most four words after it are sent. Nothing else goes with these requests.

Sharing your own ratings. The first time you rate a pair, Feelrun asks whether to share your ratings anonymously; nothing is shared before you say yes, and turning it off in Gear withdraws everything you shared. If you say yes, for each such model you rated, Feelrun sends: the model's key; the specs you entered yourself for it (weight, drop, stack heights, lug depth, membrane), not those you took from other runners or kept as an AI lookup gave them; the scores of your most reliable rating of it (comfort, grip, stability, and durability, which you rate at retirement or yourself under Something's off) and when in the pair's life you gave it; and a level from 1 to 3 for how experienced a runner you are. The level is reckoned on your iPhone from your fitness (Feelrun's VO2max estimate set against the norms for your age and sex, which Feelrun reads from Apple Health only for this, with your permission), your kilometres and how regularly you ran in the last year, and your kilometres on the shoe's ground; only the level leaves your iPhone, it only weighs your rating in the sums, and it is never shown or used for advertising. Your notes, the pair's name, size and photo, your runs, places, other Health data and anything that tells who you are are never sent. For each model your iPhone sends an identifier of its own, made from a random secret kept in its Keychain, so that you count once per model and can take your rating back, and so that your ratings of two models cannot be linked; the service keeps only a one-way hash of each identifier, the day of your last change and how many changes you made that day, and no Apple ID or device identifier. Your ratings stay until you withdraw them or the developer removes the model.

What others see. A model is shown to other runners only once three or more runners rated it, from at least three different internet connections and on at least two different days; before that, someone who asks for that model's key learns only that there are few ratings. Then only sums: for each kind of score a value and, in words, how sure it is, never a single rating, how many there are or how they spread, and the specs most runners entered. Names shown are built by Feelrun from its list of brands and the model's key. The words of a key come from the names runners gave their shoes, so the service refuses keys with offensive words or words of ads and web addresses, and every model has Report, which sends only its key. Reports are kept as a count per model and day; the developer reviews them and removes a model where needed.

Internet addresses. Like any web service, Cloudflare sees the IP address of your iPhone, and its network may log the addresses requested, which contain model keys and searches. The service itself does not store your IP address. To limit misuse it keeps, for each internet connection (an IPv6 address by its first half), only a keyed hash made with a secret key of the service, which cannot be turned back into the address without that key: per day, how many new ratings, changes and reports came from it, deleted after two days; and, per model, which connections added a runner, so that one connection cannot pose as several runners, deleted 90 days after the connection last added one or, once the model is shown to others, two days after. Every model has a hash of its own for the same connection, so nothing stored links your ratings of two models without that key; with it, the service's operator could see which models were rated from one connection in those 90 days (two days once a model is shown). Reports are kept the same way: a count per model and day, and, so that a connection reports a model once a day, a keyed mark per connection, model and day, deleted after two days. Your iPhone sends the ratings of your models one after another, so Cloudflare's network sees them come from one address at one time.

Deleting. When you withdraw, your ratings are deleted from the service at once; its database provider can restore deleted rows for 7 days (Cloudflare D1 Time Travel), after which they are gone. If you delete Feelrun after sharing and install it again, it withdraws what it shared of the models in your gear unless you say yes again.

Routes you bring in (optional)

You can bring a route into Feelrun as a GPX file, or save a loop Feelrun makes from your own trails. Feelrun keeps it on your iPhone and in your own private iCloud database, so your other devices have it; it is a route you planned, not a record of where you went, and nothing of your recorded workouts goes with it. Feelrun's developer cannot see it. When you delete a route, it is deleted from your iCloud too. A route without heights may take them from your own recorded tracks where it runs along them; that happens on the iPhone. What a route will take you, and the loops, are reckoned on the iPhone from your own workouts; nothing is sent for them.

Location

Location is used during workouts to record your route, show terrain ahead, warn when you leave a known route, and estimate sunset for daylight warnings. To name a run after the place it went to, Feelrun asks Apple's geocoder about the start and up to two more points of the run, each rounded to about 100 m. Location is not shared except as described here and above.

To make a loop "from where you are", Feelrun reads your location once, on the iPhone, when you ask; it is not stored or sent anywhere.

Motion & Fitness (barometer)

During a workout Feelrun reads the barometer (Motion & Fitness permission) to measure how much you climb. Only the resulting climb in metres is kept; pressure readings are not stored or shared. If you decline, climb is measured from GPS altitude instead.

Weather

To show conditions for a workout, Feelrun asks Apple WeatherKit for weather at the workout's location and time. For the training plan and the coach it also asks for the daily forecast near where you last ran. The location is used only to ask WeatherKit.

In the coach chat, the AI provider you chose (OpenAI or Anthropic) may search the web to answer a question about races, trails, gear or research. The search is made by the provider as part of your request to it; Feelrun sends nothing more for it.

To tell you when a newer version of Feelrun is out, the app asks Apple's App Store, at most every 12 hours, which version it has. The asking carries only the app's identifier and your iPhone's region; nothing of you or your workouts.

Photos

When you choose Save to Photos after creating a route replay video, Feelrun asks for add-only access and writes that video to your photo library. When you add a picture to a coach chat message, you pick it in the system's photo picker or paste it; Feelrun gets only that picture, keeps a scaled-down copy with the chat on your iPhone (deleted with the chat) and sends it with that message to your AI provider. A photo of a pair in Gear is taken with the camera or picked in the photo picker, and kept on your iPhone and in your private iCloud without its metadata (Gear, above); it is never sent anywhere else. Feelrun does not read your photo library.
